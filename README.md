## The two formulas people mean

NARS's concealer business runs on two products, and almost every comparison question online is about picking between them.

**Radiant Creamy Concealer** is the tube with the doe-foot wand. It's a liquid with medium, buildable coverage and what NARS calls a radiant finish — it reflects light rather than flattening it, which is why it's the usual pick for under-eyes. NARS lists it at 30 shades, with 16-hour wear, 24-hour hydration, and claims of being crease-resistant, transfer-resistant, water-resistant, and sweat- and humidity-resistant. The formula is silicone-based (water, dimethicone, hydrogenated polydecene), vegan, non-acnegenic, ophthalmologist-tested, and free of parabens, phthalates, mineral oil, retinyl palmitate, and sulfates, with under 1% synthetic fragrance.

**Soft Matte Complete Concealer** is the pot. It's a cream with full coverage and a blurred, soft-matte finish, built for spots, redness, and hyperpigmentation rather than a luminous under-eye. NARS loads it with peptides, hyaluronic acid, and a vitamin A/C/E antioxidant blend — which is worth noting if you specifically avoid vitamin A derivatives, since the Radiant Creamy ingredient callouts exclude retinyl palmitate while Soft Matte's benefit list includes it. The pot is 6.2 g, slightly more product by weight than the tube.

The practical split: liquid under the eyes, pot on blemishes. Plenty of people own both, and that's not a marketing compromise — a matte cream parked under a dry under-eye is where most of the bad reviews come from.

## How the shade codes actually work

The NARS shade system is where most people waste money, because returning a $36 concealer you picked wrong is annoying enough that many people just live with it.

Each shade carries a code: **L** for light, **M** for medium, **MD** for medium-deep, **D** for deep, followed by a number that increases with depth. Then there's the undertone, spelled out in words on the product page: neutral, warm, cool, golden, peach, or olive.

A few landmarks, from the official shade list:

- **Chantilly L1** — very light, neutral. The usual "brighten everything" pick for very fair skin.
- **Vanilla L2** — very light, neutral. Slightly deeper than Chantilly, and the one people describe as more natural-looking for everyday wear.
- **Madeleine L2.3** — light, neutral. Flagged as a bestseller on NARS's own site.
- **Café au Lait L2.4** — light, neutral with a light peach tone. Peach is a corrector's tool: it neutralizes the blue-purple cast in dark circles.
- **Custard M1** — medium, neutral. The most-requested shade in review sections, and one of the most frequently mentioned in comparisons.
- **Ginger M2** — medium, warm with a golden tone.
- **Biscuit MD1** — medium to medium-deep, warm with a golden tone.
- **Hazelnut MD4** — medium-deep to deep, cool.

NARS's own site guidance is to take the *same* shade across both concealer formulas — the two don't run meaningfully different in depth — and to move lighter or peachier only when you're specifically cancelling dark circles rather than matching skin. If Custard matches you in one formula, it matches you in the other; if you want to brighten rather than match, Cannelle (L2.75) sits a step lighter with more peach.

Two honest problems with the range. The shade names don't map to anything intuitive, so repurchasing means looking up a code you'll forget. And the codes around the light-to-medium range are packed tight — L1, L1.25, L2, L2.2, L2.3, L2.4, L2.5, L2.6 — which is great for precision and miserable for guessing online.

## Why your concealer creases

Nearly every negative review of both formulas traces back to application rather than the product itself, and the technique advice in long-form reviews converges on the same handful of moves.

Start with a hydrated under-eye. A thin layer of eye cream, given a minute or two to absorb, stops the formula from grabbing onto dry patches. Then apply small dots in a triangle under the eye instead of drawing a thick line along the lash line — the line is what creases first. Give it about ten seconds before blending so the product starts to grip, then pat rather than swipe, with a ring finger or a damp sponge. Blend outward.

Setting powder is where people overshoot. A tiny amount on the inner corner and directly under the lash line is enough; dusting the whole under-eye kills the radiance you bought the product for. For spots, the opposite approach: place the pot concealer only on the blemish with a small brush and press it in rather than blending outward, or you'll blend away the coverage.

If you have oily skin, budget for a midday check. The radiant finish reads as glow on normal and dry skin and as shine by mid-afternoon on very oily skin.

## What the verified reviews actually say

This is the part most shopping guides skip, so here it is straight.

**On Radiant Creamy**, buyer reviews on retail sites repeatedly land on the same praise: it's light, it doesn't look cakey if you use a small amount, and it does good work on dark circles with one or two thin layers. Sephora's UK listing shows a 4.46 average across more than 15,000 reviews, and NARS cites it as the top-selling prestige concealer in the US. The recurring criticisms are consistent too: coverage tops out at medium, so deep melasma, tattoos, or strong scarring won't disappear; the price-to-size ratio is poor at $36 for 6 mL; the soft-touch coating on the tube scuffs, and in humid conditions the rubberized cap gets tacky; and some buyers find the shade range leans pink or peach, leaving gaps for neutral and olive undertones.

**On Soft Matte Complete**, the split is sharper. Verified buyers who use it as a spot concealer tend to rate it highly — it stays put, it's more pigmented than the liquid, and it handles redness and post-acne marks well. The complaints come from people who tried to use the pot under their eyes: creasing that showed up by the end of the night, patchiness on dry skin, coverage that blended away to nothing on genetic dark circles, and breakouts for some acne-prone users. If your main concern is under-eyes, the liquid is the safer $36.

## Questions that come up before checkout

**Should I buy the mini first?** At $17 for 1.4 g against $36 for 6 mL, the mini costs roughly double per gram — and for the same reason, it's the cheapest way to find out whether a shade works on your skin before committing to a full tube. If you already know your shade, the full size is better value.

**Do I need both formulas?** Only if you have both problems. Under-eye dark circles and post-acne marks usually call for different textures, and the radiant finish that flatters one looks shiny on the other. If you rarely conceal spots, one tube handles it.

**Is it worth $36?** The coverage ceiling is real — this is a medium-coverage concealer, and no amount of layering turns it into full coverage. What you're paying for is the finish, the shade precision across 30 codes, and the wear time. If you want maximum coverage per dollar, the pot is a better deal by weight; if you want a concealer that doesn't announce itself, the tube earns its price.
